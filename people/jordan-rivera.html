How I work

How I use AI

I treat AI as a fast pair-programmer, not an autopilot. My rhythm is to write the spec and the interface first, then use Claude to scaffold the implementation and to rubber-duck the tricky logic — the buzz-timing race condition on Owl Jeopardy took a few rounds of "here's what's happening, what am I missing?" before it clicked.

Where it saves me the most time is the boring-but-necessary work: writing tests, converting a rough function into clean typed code, and drafting the first version of docs. I still read every line before it ships.

"AI got me to a working prototype fast — but understanding why it worked was still on me. That's the part that made me a better engineer."

Proud of

A hard problem I cracked

On Owl Jeopardy, two players buzzing a fraction of a second apart were sometimes both scored as "first." I traced it to us trusting the client's clock. The fix — timestamp every buzz on the server the instant it arrives, and resolve ties there — was small in code but took real digging to find. It's the moment the project stopped feeling like a class assignment and started feeling like software.
